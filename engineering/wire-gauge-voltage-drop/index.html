Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
V_drop = (2 · K · I · L) / Circular_Mils | % Drop = (V_drop / V_supply) · 100

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. Ohm's Law Conductor Resistance

Total circuit conductor loop resistance depends on specific resistivity K (12.9 for copper, 21.2 for aluminum) and one-way distance L: R = (2 · K · L) / Circular_Mils.

2
2. Voltage Drop Formulation

Single-phase and DC voltage drop: V_drop = I · R = (2 · K · I · L) / cmil. For three-phase balanced circuits, the factor 2 is replaced by √3 ≈ 1.732.

3
3. NEC Sizing Invariant

To keep percentage drop ≤ 3%, solve for circular mils: cmil_required = (2 · K · I · L) / (V_supply · 0.03). Then select the nearest standard AWG wire gauge that meets or exceeds this cross-sectional area.

Frequently Asked Questions

What wire gauge should I use for 100 ft at 20 amps on 120V? +
For a 120V, 20-amp circuit running 100 feet, standard 12 AWG copper wire produces a 3.95% voltage drop (exceeding the NEC 3% recommendation). Upgrading to 10 AWG copper drops the voltage drop to 2.49% (below 3%), delivering 117.0 Volts at the load.
Why does the NEC recommend a maximum 3% voltage drop? +
Excessive voltage drop causes electric motors to overheat and burn out prematurely, incandescent and LED lights to flicker or dim, and electronic power supplies to malfunction.
Why is copper better than aluminum for voltage drop? +
Copper has roughly 60% lower electrical resistance than aluminum for the same cross-sectional area (K=12.9 vs K=21.2). An aluminum conductor must be approximately two AWG sizes larger than copper to carry the same current with identical voltage drop.

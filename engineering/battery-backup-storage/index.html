Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
Usable kWh = Daily kWh · Days | Nameplate kWh = Usable / (DoD · InverterEff) | Ah = (kWh · 1000) / VDC

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. Net Usable Storage Requirement

Multiply daily essential kilowatt-hours by target days of autonomy: Usable kWh = Daily_kWh × Days_of_Autonomy.

2
2. Depth of Discharge (DoD) & Inverter Derating

Batteries cannot be 100% discharged without permanent chemistry damage. Divide usable kWh by allowable DoD (0.90 for LiFePO4, 0.50 for lead-acid) and inverter efficiency (0.92): Nameplate kWh = Usable_kWh / (DoD × Inverter_Eff).

3
3. Amp-Hour (Ah) Conversion

By electrical definition: Power (Watts) = Volts × Amps. Convert nameplate kWh to Amp-Hours at battery DC bus voltage: Ah = (Nameplate_kWh × 1,000) / VDC.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many kWh battery backup does a typical house need? +
To power critical circuits (refrigerator, lights, internet, well pump, gas furnace blower) during an outage, an average home needs between 10 kWh and 15 kWh per day. A single 13.5 kWh Tesla Powerwall or two 5.12 kWh server rack batteries provides ~1 day of essential autonomy.
Why is LiFePO4 preferred over lead-acid batteries for home backup? +
LiFePO4 (Lithium Iron Phosphate) can be safely discharged to 90% without capacity loss, delivers over 4,000 to 6,000 charge cycles, and does not off-gas toxic hydrogen. Lead-acid batteries must stop at 50% discharge and last only 500 to 800 cycles.
How do you calculate Amp-Hours (Ah) from kWh? +
Multiply the capacity in kilowatt-hours by 1,000 to find watt-hours, then divide by the DC system voltage: Ah = (kWh × 1000) / Volts. For example, 10 kWh on a 48V system is (10,000 / 48) = 208.3 Amp-Hours.

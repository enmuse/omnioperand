Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. Baseline Heat Loss Formulation

ASHRAE rule-of-thumb baseline heating capacity assigns 30 to 55 BTU/hr per square foot depending on local 99% winter dry-bulb design temperatures.

2
2. Volume & Envelope Modifiers

Ceilings above 8 feet increase thermal room air volume proportionally. Building envelope factors range from 0.85 for tight modern homes to 1.20 for drafty historic structures.

3
3. AFUE Efficiency Inversion

Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency (AFUE) measures burner combustion conversion. A furnace delivering 60,000 BTU/hr output at 80% AFUE requires an input rating of 60,000 / 0.80 = 75,000 BTU/hr.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size furnace do I need for a 2,000 sq ft house? +
In a moderate Zone 3 climate with average insulation, a 2,000 sq ft home with 8ft ceilings requires approximately 80,000 BTU/hr of heating output. An 80% AFUE furnace would need an input rating of 100,000 BTU/hr, while a 96% AFUE unit needs ~83,000 BTU/hr.
What happens if a furnace is oversized for a house? +
Oversized furnaces cause 'short-cycling'—turning on and off rapidly. This creates severe indoor temperature swings, fails to dehumidify or filter air properly, increases mechanical wear on the heat exchanger, and spikes electrical bills.
How do you convert furnace BTUs to tons? +
One ton of heating or air conditioning capacity equals exactly 12,000 BTU/hr. Divide your total heat loss BTU by 12,000 to find the equivalent tonnage (e.g. 60,000 BTU = 5.0 Tons).

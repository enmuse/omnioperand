Analytical Derivation & Methodology

1
1. Apparent Power from Active Load

In AC systems, apparent power S (in kVA) accounts for reactive currents: S_load = P_kW / PowerFactor. Applying the continuous duty safety margin gives design capacity: S_design = S_load × (1 + Margin%).

2
2. Three-Phase Voltage & Current Relation

For a balanced 3-phase system, apparent power is S = √3 × V_LL × I_line. Inverting for full-load amperes yields: FLA = (kVA × 1,000) / (√3 × V_LL), where √3 ≈ 1.7320508.

3
3. NEC Standard Rating Selection & Heat Dissipation

Commercial dry-type transformers follow standard NEMA/NEC step ratings: 15, 30, 45, 75, 112.5, 150, 225, 300, 500 kVA. Core hysteresis and I²R winding resistance dissipate approx 1.5% to 2.5% of rated capacity as heat under full load.

Frequently Asked Questions

To find Amps from 3-phase kVA: Amps = (kVA × 1,000) / (1.732 × Voltage). For example, a 75 kVA transformer at 208V delivers: (75 × 1,000) / (1.732 × 208) = 208.2 Amperes.

In a 3-phase system, the line-to-line voltage is vectorially 120° out of phase with line-to-neutral voltage. The geometric vector difference between any two phases is 2 × sin(60°) = √3 ≈ 1.732.

Assuming an 0.85 power factor, 100 kW equals 117.6 kVA. Adding a 25% continuous duty factor requires 147 kVA. The nearest standard commercial transformer rating is 150 kVA.

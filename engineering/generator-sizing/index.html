Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
Running kW = Σ W_run / 1000 | Peak Surge kW = Running kW + max(W_surge - W_run) / 1000 | kVA = kW / 0.80

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. Inductive vs Resistive Loads

Electric motors (refrigerators, AC compressors, well pumps) require an inductive inrush surge of 2 to 3 times their running wattage for 2–3 seconds to break rotor inertia.

2
2. Coincident Surge Principle

Motors rarely start at the exact same millisecond. Peak starting load equals the total continuous running watts of all appliances plus the single largest motor starting surge.

3
3. Power Factor & Safety Headroom

Generators are rated in both kW (real power) and kVA (apparent power) assuming a standard 0.80 lagging power factor: kVA = kW / 0.80. Adding a 20% margin prevents voltage sagging and frequency distortion.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size generator do I need to run a whole house? +
To power essential appliances (refrigerator, well pump, lights, furnace blower, microwave), a 7,500 to 10,000 Watt (7.5–10 kW) portable generator is sufficient. To run a central 3-to-4 ton central air conditioner alongside the house, a 14 kW to 22 kW whole-home standby generator is required.
What is the difference between running watts and starting watts? +
Running (continuous) watts is the electrical power required to keep an appliance operating. Starting (surge) watts is the extra power needed for 2 to 3 seconds to start electric motor compressors and pumps.
How do you convert generator kW to kVA? +
Standard AC generators operate at an industry-standard 0.8 power factor. Divide the real power in kW by 0.8 to calculate kVA (for example: 10 kW / 0.8 = 12.5 kVA).

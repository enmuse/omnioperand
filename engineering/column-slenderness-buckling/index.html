Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. Effective Slenderness Ratio

Slenderness measures column susceptibility to geometric buckling: λ = (K × L × 12) / r. The maximum recommended slenderness under AISC 360 is λ ≤ 200.

2
2. Euler Critical Buckling Stress

For an ideal elastic column with modulus E = 29,000 ksi: F_e = (π² × E) / λ².

3
3. Inelastic vs Elastic AISC 360 Transition

The transition slenderness threshold is 4.71 × √(E / Fy) (approx 113.8 for 50 ksi steel). Below this limit, inelastic buckling and residual stresses govern: Fcr = [0.658^(Fy/Fe)] × Fy. Above this limit, elastic buckling governs: Fcr = 0.877 × Fe.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good slenderness ratio for structural columns? +
AISC 360 recommends an upper limit of KL/r ≤ 200 for compression members. Highly efficient main building columns typically operate at KL/r between 40 and 90.
Why does a column buckle about its weak axis? +
Buckling occurs about the axis with the smallest moment of inertia and least radius of gyration (r_min), as this requires the lowest strain energy to deform.
What is the K factor in column design? +
K is the effective length factor representing the distance between inflection points of curvature. A column pinned at both ends has K=1.0, while fixed-fixed ends cut effective length in half (K=0.5).

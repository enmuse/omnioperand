Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Thermal Heat Ingress Driving Sublimation

Dry ice sublimates at -109.3°F (-78.5°C). Thermal Gradient ΔT = Ambient (75°F) - (-109.3°F) = 184.3°F.

2
Insulation Heat Influx Rate

2-inch EPS foam provides R-8 thermal resistance. Heat flux q = Area × ΔT / R. A standard 1.5 cu ft insulated shipper absorbs ~55 BTU/hr.

3
Latent Heat of Sublimation

Dry ice latent heat of sublimation = 246 BTU/lb. Hourly loss = 55 / 246 = 0.224 lbs/hr (5.37 lbs/day).

4
Safety Cushion Charge

72 hours × 0.224 lbs/hr = 16.1 lbs + 20% safety reserve = 19.3 lbs initial dry ice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can dry ice packages be sealed airtight? +
NEVER seal a dry ice container airtight! Dry ice sublimates directly from solid to gas, expanding ~845 times in volume. Sealing it in a gas-tight container creates a dangerous explosion hazard.
What is the IATA Dangerous Goods regulation for dry ice? +
Dry ice is classified as UN 1845, Class 9 Miscellaneous Dangerous Goods. Shipments require Class 9 hazard diamond labels and declaration of net dry ice weight in kilograms on airway bills.

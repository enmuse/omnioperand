Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
LTL Aggregate Cost Formulation

Total LTL Cost = Pallet Count × Rate per Pallet. 8 pallets × $245 = $1,960.00.

2
FTL Breakeven Derivation

N_breakeven = FTL Flat Rate / LTL Cost per Pallet. $2,150 / $245 = 8.78 pallets.

3
Cost Decision Threshold

If shipping ≤ 8 pallets, LTL is cheaper. At 9 pallets or more ($2,205 LTL vs $2,150 FTL), booking a dedicated FTL trailer saves money.

4
Speed & Damage Secondary Tradeoff

FTL moves directly dock-to-dock with zero transshipment terminals, eliminating transit delays and cross-dock forklift damage risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many pallets fit in a standard 53-foot dry van trailer? +
A 53ft dry van holds 26 single-stacked 48x40 pallets (straight loading) or up to 30 pallets with turned/pinwheeled loading. Double-stacking increases capacity to 52 pallets if weight allows.
What is Volume LTL / Partial Truckload? +
Volume LTL (or partial truckload) bridges the gap for shipments of 6 to 14 pallets that are too large for standard LTL hub-and-spoke networks but don't justify a full dedicated truckload.

Analytical Proof & Invariants

Governing Mathematical Formula:
Cone: s = √(r² + h²) | Pyramid: s = √[h² + (a/2)²] | Frustum Cone: s = √[h² + (R - r)²]

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. Right Cone Slant Height Formulation

The slant height s forms the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle where the vertical height h is the altitude and the base radius r is the adjacent base: s = √(r² + h²).

2
2. Regular Square Pyramid Slant Height & Corner Edges

For a pyramid with square base a and vertical height h, the apothem of the base is a/2. The face slant height is s = √[h² + (a/2)²]. The corner edge length is e = √[s² + (a/2)²] = √[h² + a²/2].

3
3. Truncated Frustum Generalization

For a truncated frustum with bottom radius R and top radius r, cutting through the central axis yields a right trapezoid. The radial delta is Δr = (R - r). Applying the Pythagorean theorem yields: s = √[h² + (R - r)²].

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the slant height of a cone or pyramid? +
The slant height is the distance measured along the lateral face from the apex down to the outer perimeter of the base. For a right cone, it equals the hypotenuse of the triangle formed by radius and height.
What is the difference between vertical height and slant height? +
Vertical height (h) is the perpendicular altitude from the center of the base straight to the top apex. Slant height (s) is always longer than vertical height because s = √(r² + h²).
How do you find slant height of a truncated cone (frustum)? +
Subtract the smaller top radius from the larger base radius to find the horizontal offset Δr = R - r, then apply Pythagoras: s = √[h² + (R - r)²].

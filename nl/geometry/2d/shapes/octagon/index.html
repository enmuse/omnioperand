Mathematical Foundations & Analytical Derivations

A regular octagon is a two-dimensional convex regular polygon with 8 equal sides and 8 equal interior angles. It belongs to the dihedral symmetry group D8, featuring 8 axes of reflective symmetry and 8-fold rotational symmetry (45° rotational invariance).

1 Interior, Exterior & Central Angles

▼
a
Sum of Interior Angles: For any convex n-gon, the sum of interior angles is given by S = (n - 2) × 180°. For an octagon (n = 8):
S = (8 - 2) × 180° = 6 × 180° = 1080°   (6π rad)
b
Individual Interior Angle (α): Because the polygon is regular, all 8 angles are congruent:
α = 1080° / 8 = 135°   (3π / 4 rad)
c
Central & Exterior Angle (β): Dividing a full revolution (360°) equally among 8 sectors yields:
β = 360° / 8 = 45°   (π / 4 rad)
The half-central angle subtending each right triangle is θ = 45° / 2 = 22.5°   (π / 8 rad).

2 Apothem (Inradius) Exact Radical Derivation

▼

The apothem ri is the perpendicular distance from the geometric center to the midpoint of any side. In the right triangle formed by the center, a vertex, and the side midpoint:

tan(22.5°) = (a / 2) / ri ⟹ ri = a / [2 · tan(22.5°)]

Using the tangent half-angle trigonometric identity tan(θ / 2) = sin(θ) / [1 + cos(θ)] with θ = 45°:

tan(22.5°) = (√2 / 2) / (1 + √2 / 2) = √2 / (2 + √2) = [√2(2 - √2)] / (4 - 2) = √2 - 1

Substituting this into the apothem formula and rationalizing the denominator:

ri = a / [2(√2 - 1)] = [a(√2 + 1)] / [2(2 - 1)] = [a(1 + √2)] / 2 ≈ 1.20710678 · a

3 Circumradius Exact Radical Derivation

▼

The circumradius R is the distance from the center to any vertex:

sin(22.5°) = (a / 2) / R ⟹ R = a / [2 · sin(22.5°)]

Applying the sine half-angle identity sin(22.5°) = √[(1 - cos(45°)) / 2] = √[(1 - √2/2) / 2] = √(2 - √2) / 2:

R = a / [2 · (√(2 - √2) / 2)] = a / √(2 - √2) = [a√(2 + √2)] / √(4 - 2) = [a√(4 + 2√2)] / 2 ≈ 1.30656296 · a

4 Area Derivation: Four Distinct Proof Methods

▼
A
Method 1: Sum of 8 Isosceles Triangles The octagon can be partitioned into 8 congruent isosceles triangles, each having base a and height equal to apothem ri:
A = 8 × (½ · a · ri) = 4a · [a(1 + √2) / 2] = 2(1 + √2)a² ≈ 4.82842712 · a²
B
Method 2: Bounding Square Subtraction (Carpentry Cut-Off Method) Enclose the octagon in a tight bounding square. Each of the 4 corners is an isosceles right triangle with hypotenuse a. The legs of each corner triangle are x = a / √2. The total side length of the bounding square is:
S = a + 2x = a + 2(a / √2) = a(1 + √2)
The area of the square is S² = a²(1 + √2)² = a²(3 + 2√2). The total area of the 4 corner triangles is 4 × (½ x²) = 2(a² / 2) = a². Subtracting the 4 corners:
A = S² - a² = a²(3 + 2√2) - a² = 2(1 + √2)a²
C
Method 3: From Flat-to-Flat Span (S) Because S = a(1 + √2) ⟹ a = S / (1 + √2) = S(√2 - 1):
A = 2(1 + √2)[S(√2 - 1)]² = 2(1 + √2)(3 - 2√2)S² = 2(√2 - 1)S² ≈ 0.82842712 · S²
D
Method 4: From Circumradius (R) Using the triangle area formula with central angle β = 45°:
A = 8 × [½ R² sin(45°)] = 4R² · (√2 / 2) = 2√2 · R² ≈ 2.82842712 · R²

5 The 3 Distinct Diagonal Classes & Law of Cosines

▼

An n-gon has n(n - 3) / 2 diagonals. For n = 8, there are exactly 8(5) / 2 = 20 diagonals, grouped into three distinct congruence classes:

1
Short Diagonal (dS, 8 total): Connects vertices separated by 1 vertex. In the triangle formed by two adjacent sides of length a and interior angle 135°, apply the Law of Cosines:
dS² = a² + a² - 2a² cos(135°) = 2a² - 2a²(-√2 / 2) = a²(2 + √2)
dS = a · √(2 + √2) ≈ 1.84775907 · a
2
Medium Diagonal / Span (S, 8 total): Connects vertices across two intervening sides. This equals the flat-to-flat height:
S = 2ri = a(1 + √2) ≈ 2.41421356 · a
3
Long Diagonal / Diameter (dL, 4 total): Connects directly opposite vertices through the polygon center. This is the diameter of the circumcircle:
dL = 2R = a · √(4 + 2√2) ≈ 2.61312593 · a

Veelgestelde Vragen

What is the formula for the area of a regular octagon? +
The area of a regular octagon with side length a is A = 2(1 + √2)a² ≈ 4.828427a². If you know the inradius (apothem) ri, the area is A = 8(√2 - 1)ri² ≈ 3.313708ri². If you know the flat-to-flat span S, the area is A = 2(√2 - 1)S² ≈ 0.828427S².
How do you calculate the side length from the area of an octagon? +
To solve for side length a from area A, invert the formula: a = √[A / (2(1 + √2))] = √[A(√2 - 1) / 2] ≈ 0.4550898 · √A.
How do you find the three diagonal lengths of an octagon? +
A regular octagon has three distinct diagonal lengths: 1. Short diagonal (dS): dS = a · √(2 + √2) ≈ 1.84776a 2. Medium diagonal / Span (S): S = a(1 + √2) ≈ 2.41421a 3. Long diagonal (dL): dL = a · √(4 + 2√2) ≈ 2.61313a
What miter angle should I cut on a miter saw to build an octagon? +
Each interior angle of a regular octagon is 135°. Because two pieces of wood meet at each corner, divide the corner angle in half: 135° / 2 = 67.5°. Since miter saws measure angles relative to a 90° square cut, set your saw to 22.5° (90° - 67.5° = 22.5°).

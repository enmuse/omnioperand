Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
Stopping Distance = (Speed × Reaction Time) + (Speed² / [2 × g × Friction Coefficient μ])

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Driver Perception-Reaction Distance

Converting speed to feet per second: v (ft/s) = MPH × 1.46667. Reaction Distance d_react = v × Reaction Time.

2
Kinetic Energy Dissipation Braking

Braking Distance d_brake = v² / (2 · g · μ), where g = 32.174 ft/s² and μ is the tire-to-road friction coefficient.

3
Quadratic Velocity Scaling

Braking distance scales with the SQUARE of speed: doubling your speed quadruples your braking distance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is stopping distance so much longer on wet roads? +
Wet asphalt drops tire friction coefficient μ from ~0.80 down to ~0.45, nearly doubling the mechanical braking distance. On packed snow (μ ≈ 0.20), stopping distance increases four-fold.
What is the 3-second following distance rule? +
At 65 MPH, you travel 95 feet every single second. A 3-second gap provides 285 feet of safety buffer, allowing enough space for perception, reaction, and smooth braking.

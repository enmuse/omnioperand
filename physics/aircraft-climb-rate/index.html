Governing Mathematical Formula:
Climb Gradient (ft/NM) = (FPM × 60) / Groundspeed (Knots) | Gradient % = ft/NM / 60.76

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Kinematic Motion Across Ground

One nautical mile equals 6,076.12 feet. At groundspeed GS knots, an aircraft travels GS / 60 nautical miles per minute.

2
Climb Gradient Formulation

Gradient (ft/NM) = Vertical Speed (FPM) / Nautical Miles per Minute = (FPM × 60) / GS.

3
Obstacle Clearance Standard

Standard instrument departure procedures (TERPS) mandate a minimum climb gradient of 200 ft/NM (3.3%) to ensure obstacle clearance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does a headwind increase your climb gradient? +
A headwind reduces your groundspeed without changing your aerodynamic rate of climb (FPM). Since you are moving forward across the ground more slowly, you gain more altitude per nautical mile, clearing runway obstacles more easily.
What is the standard FAA climb gradient? +
Unless published otherwise, the standard FAA climb gradient is 200 feet per nautical mile (approximately 3.3% gradient).

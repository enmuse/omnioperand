Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Work-Energy Theorem Derivation

Work done on a mass is the line integral of force: W = ∫ F dx = ∫ (m · dv/dt) dx = m ∫ v dv = ½ · m · v².

2
Quadratic Velocity Scaling

Because velocity is squared, doubling vehicle speed quadruples (4×) required braking distance and collision impact energy.

3
Momentum Relationship

Linear momentum p = mv. Kinetic energy can be expressed via momentum as E_k = p² / (2m). 1 Joule = 0.737562 ft-lbs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does braking distance quadruple when speed doubles? +
Brakes perform mechanical work against kinetic energy (W = F · d = ½mv²). Since kinetic energy scales quadratically with velocity (v²), doubling your speed from 30 mph to 60 mph multiplies kinetic energy by 4, requiring 4 times the stopping distance.
When does relativistic kinetic energy become necessary? +
Classical Newtonian kinetic energy (½mv²) is accurate for speeds below ~10% the speed of light. Above 0.1c, Einstein's relativistic equation E_k = (γ - 1)mc² must be used to account for Lorentz factor γ.

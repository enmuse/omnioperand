Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
James Watt Horsepower Definition

One mechanical horsepower HP = 33,000 foot-pounds of work per minute = 550 ft-lb/sec.

2
Rotational Work Angular Velocity

Power = Torque × Angular Velocity = Torque (lb-ft) × [2π × RPM / 60] / 550. Combining constants: 550 × 60 / (2π) = 33,000 / 2π = 5,252.113.

3
The 5,252 RPM Dyno Invariant

On every dyno chart on Earth plotting horsepower and torque in lb-ft on identical scales, the curves MUST cross precisely at 5,252 RPM: HP ≡ Torque at 5,252 RPM.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do horsepower and torque curves always cross at 5,252 RPM? +
Because of unit definitions! Since 1 HP is defined as 33,000 ft-lb/min, and one revolution equals 2π radians, the constant is 33,000 / 2π = 5,252. At 5,252 RPM, HP = (Torque × 5,252) / 5,252 = Torque.
What is the difference between horsepower and torque? +
Torque is instantaneous twisting force (how much work the engine can do). Horsepower is how rapidly that twisting force is applied over time (how fast work gets done). Torque gives you acceleration off the line; horsepower delivers top-end speed.

Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
SFM = (π · D_in · RPM) / 12 ≈ (D_in · RPM) / 3.82 | Metric vc = (π · D_mm · RPM) / 1000 | g = (ω² · r) / 9.80665

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. Circumferential Velocity Invariant

Each rotation sweeps through perimeter distance π × D. In inches per minute, speed is π × D × RPM. Dividing by 12 converts to Surface Feet per Minute: SFM = (π × D × RPM) / 12 ≈ (D × RPM) / 3.8197.

2
2. Metric Cutting Speed (vc)

In metric ISO 3002 conventions with diameter in millimeters: vc (m/min) = (π × D_mm × RPM) / 1,000.

3
3. Centrifugal Rim G-Force

With angular velocity ω = (2π × RPM) / 60 rad/s, the centrifugal acceleration experienced at the outer rim is a_c = ω² × r. Dividing by standard gravity g = 9.80665 m/s² yields relative g-force.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SFM in machining? +
SFM stands for Surface Feet per Minute. It is the cutting speed at which the outer edge of a tool passes over the workpiece material, determining tool life and heat generation.
How do you calculate RPM from SFM? +
Use the standard machinist rule-of-thumb: RPM = (SFM × 3.82) / Diameter. For example, machining aluminum at 600 SFM with a 0.5-inch end mill requires (600 × 3.82) / 0.5 = 4,584 RPM.
Why is circumferential speed critical for grinding wheels? +
Grinding wheels have strict maximum peripheral surface speeds (typically 6,000 to 8,500 SFM). Exceeding this limit generates destructive centrifugal stresses that can cause wheel explosion.

Governing Mathematical Formula:
Cruising Range (NM) = Usable Fuel (Gallons) × (Speed in Knots / GPH)

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Marine Fuel Efficiency NMPG

Nautical Miles per Gallon NMPG = Speed (Knots) / Fuel Burn (GPH). For example: 24 knots / 18 GPH = 1.33 NMPG.

2
Coast Guard 10% Reserve Rule

Mariners must maintain at least a 10% (daytime coastal) to 33% (offshore passage) fuel safety reserve: Usable Fuel = Tank Capacity × 0.90.

3
Safe Range Formulation

Safe Range (NM) = Usable Gallons × NMPG. Converting to Statute Miles: Statute Miles = NM × 1.15078.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Rule of Thirds in marine fuel planning? +
The offshore Rule of Thirds states: 1/3 of fuel for the trip out, 1/3 for the return voyage, and 1/3 held in reserve for unexpected headwinds, heavy seas, or navigation diversions.
How does boat hull type affect fuel burn? +
Planing hulls burn substantial fuel while climbing over their bow wave, achieving maximum efficiency once fully up on plane. Displacement hulls have low drag at low speeds, but encounter an impenetrable hull speed barrier where fuel burn spikes exponentially.

Mathematical Foundations & Hexagonal Symmetries

The regular hexagon has 6 congruent sides and 6 congruent interior angles. It belongs to the dihedral symmetry group D6 with 6 reflection axes and 6-fold rotational symmetry (60° invariance). It is one of only three regular polygons capable of completely tessellating the 2D Euclidean plane without gaps.

1 Interior, Exterior & Central Angles

▼
a
Sum of Interior Angles: For n = 6:
S = (6 - 2) × 180° = 4 × 180° = 720°   (4π rad)
b
Individual Interior Angle (α):
α = 720° / 6 = 120°   (2π / 3 rad)
c
Central & Exterior Angle (β):
β = 360° / 6 = 60°   (π / 3 rad)

2 Proof that Circumradius (R) Equals Side Length (a)

▼

Connect the center O to any two adjacent vertices V_1 and V_2. The central angle is ∠V_1OV_2 = 60°. Since both radii legs are equal (OV_1 = OV_2 = R), the triangle ΔOV_1V_2 is isosceles.

Base angles = (180° - 60°) / 2 = 60°

Because all three angles are 60°, the triangle is equilateral. Therefore:

R = a

3 Apothem (Inradius) Radical Derivation

▼

The apothem ri is the altitude of one of the 6 central equilateral triangles:

ri² + (a / 2)² = a² ⟹ ri² = a² - a² / 4 = 3a² / 4
ri = (a · √3) / 2 ≈ 0.86602540 · a

4 Area Exact Radical Derivations

▼

A regular hexagon comprises exactly 6 equilateral triangles of side a:

A = 6 × (a²√3 / 4) = (3√3 / 2)a² ≈ 2.59807621 · a²

In terms of the flat-to-flat span S = 2ri = a√3 ⟹ a = S / √3:

A = (3√3 / 2) · (S / √3)² = (√3 / 2)S² ≈ 0.86602540 · S²

5 The 2 Distinct Diagonal Classes

▼

An n-gon has n(n - 3) / 2 diagonals. For n = 6, there are 6(3) / 2 = 9 diagonals in two congruence classes:

1
Short Diagonal (dS, 6 total): Connects alternate vertices across 2 sides. Applying the Law of Cosines with interior angle 120°:
dS² = a² + a² - 2a² cos(120°) = 2a² - 2a²(-½) = 3a²
dS = a√3 ≈ 1.73205081 · a
2
Long Diagonal (dL, 3 total): Connects directly opposite vertices through the center. It equals the circumcircle diameter:
dL = 2R = 2a

What is the formula for the area of a regular hexagon? +
The area of a regular hexagon with side length a is A = (3√3 / 2)a² ≈ 2.598076a². If you know the inradius (apothem) ri, the area is A = 2√3 · ri² ≈ 3.464102ri². If you know the flat-to-flat span S, the area is A = (√3 / 2)S² ≈ 0.866025S².
Why is the circumradius equal to the side length? +
A regular hexagon is formed by 6 congruent equilateral triangles meeting at the center. Because all sides of an equilateral triangle are equal, the distance from the center to any vertex (Circumradius R) is always strictly equal to the outer side length a.
What are the two diagonal lengths of a regular hexagon? +
A regular hexagon has 9 total diagonals in 2 distinct classes: 1. Short diagonals (dS): dS = a√3 ≈ 1.73205a (6 diagonals connecting vertices separated by one vertex) 2. Long diagonals (dL): dL = 2a (3 diagonals passing through the center connecting opposite vertices)
What are the socket and wrench sizes for a hex bolt? +
Wrench and socket sizes measure the flat-to-flat span (S), which equals 2ri = a√3. For example, a bolt with a side length of 10 mm requires a socket size of 10√3 ≈ 17.32 mm (typically a standard 17mm or 18mm socket).

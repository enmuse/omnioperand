Governing Mathematical Formula:
MAP = Diastolic BP + [1/3 × (Systolic BP - Diastolic BP)]

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Time-Weighted Cardiac Pressure Integral

Because the human heart spends approximately two-thirds of the cardiac cycle in diastole and one-third in systole at resting heart rates, the arithmetic mean is weighted: MAP = [SBP + (2 × DBP)] / 3 = DBP + ⅓(SBP - DBP).

2
Pulse Pressure Invariant

Pulse Pressure PP = SBP - DBP. Pulse pressures consistently above 60 mmHg reflect increased arterial stiffness and cardiovascular disease risk.

3
Critical Organ Perfusion Threshold

In critical care medicine, a MAP of at least 65 mmHg is mandatory to maintain adequate capillary perfusion to vital organs (kidneys, brain, coronary arteries).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is considered a normal MAP range? +
A normal resting MAP is between 70 and 100 mmHg. A MAP consistently below 65 mmHg indicates hypoperfusion/shock, while a MAP above 105 mmHg indicates hypertension.
Why is MAP more clinically useful than systolic blood pressure alone? +
Systolic pressure fluctuates dramatically with stress, caffeine, and arterial wave reflections. MAP represents the true steady-state driving pressure delivering oxygenated blood across microvascular capillary beds.

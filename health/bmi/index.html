Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Quetelet Index Derivation

BMI was formulated in 1832 by Adolphe Quetelet: BMI = Mass (kg) / Height (m)². In imperial units: BMI = [Weight (lbs) / Height (in)²] × 703.0695.

2
WHO Diagnostic Cutoffs

Underweight: < 18.5; Normal: 18.5–24.9; Overweight: 25.0–29.9; Class I Obesity: 30.0–34.9; Class II: 35.0–39.9; Class III: ≥ 40.0.

3
BMI Prime Normalization

BMI Prime = BMI / 25.0. An individual with BMI Prime < 1.0 is within the non-overweight spectrum.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is BMI inaccurate for muscular athletes and bodybuilders? +
BMI does not distinguish between skeletal muscle mass and adipose fat tissue. Muscular athletes often have high BMI scores that falsely categorize them as overweight or obese despite low body fat percentages.
What is a healthy BMI for adults? +
For adults 20 and older, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is categorized as normal or healthy by the CDC and World Health Organization.

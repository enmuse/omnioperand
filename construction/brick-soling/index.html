Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. Effective Face Area Formulation

For modular flat soling (8" × 4"), each brick covers 32 sq in (0.222 sq ft), yielding ~4.5 bricks/sq ft. In brick-on-edge (8" × 2.25"), each brick covers 18 sq in (0.125 sq ft), requiring ~8.0 bricks/sq ft.

2
2. Sand Cushion Volume

Bedding sand volume equals Surface Area × (Depth in inches / 12). Divide cubic feet by 27 to calculate cubic yards. Multiply cubic yards by ~1.35 tons/yd³ for compacted sand mass.

3
3. Sub-base Structural Role

Brick soling serves as a rigid foundation layer over compacted subgrade beneath concrete floor slabs or flexible road pavements, preventing moisture capillary rise and distributing point loads.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is brick soling? +
Brick soling is a traditional foundation engineering technique where a single or double layer of bricks is laid flat or on-edge over compacted earth or sand to create a stable, non-yielding base for concrete slabs or road pavements.
How many bricks are needed for 100 sq ft of flat soling? +
Using standard modular bricks (8" × 4"), you need approximately 450 bricks for 100 sq ft (net). Adding a 5% cutting waste brings the total to 473 bricks.
Why is sand bedding used under soling? +
A 1 to 2-inch cushion of clean coarse sand levels the subgrade, absorbs minor settlement variations, and provides drainage beneath the bricks.

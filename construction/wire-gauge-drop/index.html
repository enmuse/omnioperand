Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
Voltage Drop (VD) = [2 × K × I × Length (ft)] / Circular Mils (CM)

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Ohm's Law for Long Run Conductors

Voltage Drop V_d = 2 × K × I × L / CM, where K = 12.9 ohms-cmil/ft for copper at 75°C, I is current in amps, L is one-way distance in feet, and CM is circular mils.

2
Conductor Circular Mil Cross-Sections

14 AWG = 4,110 CM; 12 AWG = 6,530 CM; 10 AWG = 10,380 CM; 8 AWG = 16,510 CM; 6 AWG = 26,240 CM; 4 AWG = 41,740 CM.

3
NEC Maximum Drop Recommendation

National Electrical Code (NEC) Section 210.19(A) recommends that voltage drop not exceed 3% on branch circuits and 5% total across feeder and branch combined.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is high voltage drop dangerous? +
Excessive voltage drop causes electric motors to overheat and burn out, dimming lights, sensitive electronic tripping, and wasted energy dissipated as heat inside wall conduits.
When should I upsize from 12 AWG to 10 AWG on a 20A circuit? +
Whenever a 120V 20A circuit run exceeds 70 to 80 feet, upsizing from 12 AWG to 10 AWG keeps voltage drop below the NEC recommended 3% threshold.

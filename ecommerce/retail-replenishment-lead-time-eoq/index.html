Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
EOQ = √[(2 · D · S) / H] | Total Cost = (D / Q) · S + (Q / 2) · H

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
EOQ Square Root Derivation

EOQ = √[(2 × D × S) / H] = √[(2 × 12,000 × $150) / $3.60] = √[3,600,000 / 3.60] = √1,000,000 = 1,000 units.

2
Annual Purchase Order Frequency

Orders Per Year = D / EOQ = 12,000 / 1,000 = 12 orders per year.

3
Annual Ordering Cost

Annual Ordering Cost = 12 orders × $150.00 = $1,800.00.

4
Annual Carrying Holding Cost

Average Inventory = EOQ / 2 = 500 units. Holding Cost = 500 × $3.60 = $1,800.00. Total Cost = $1,800 + $1,800 = $3,600.00 (Ordering costs exactly equal holding costs at the EOQ minimum).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Ford W. Harris Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) formula? +
Published in 1913, EOQ balances ordering costs (which decrease per unit as batch size rises) against holding costs (which increase linearly as batch size rises). The optimal order size occurs where ordering costs equal holding costs.
What happens if a supplier has a Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ) higher than EOQ? +
If supplier MOQ exceeds your EOQ, you are forced to order at the MOQ, accepting higher inventory holding costs to meet factory production requirements.

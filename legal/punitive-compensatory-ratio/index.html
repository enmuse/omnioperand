SCOTUS Landmark Precedents

In State Farm v. Campbell (2003) and BMW v. Gore (1996), the U.S. Supreme Court held that punitive awards exceeding a single-digit ratio (9:1) violate 14th Amendment Due Process, and that ratios exceeding 4:1 are constitutionally suspect when compensatory damages are substantial.

3

Frequently Asked Questions

Can punitive damages ever exceed a 9:1 ratio constitutionally? +
Yes, but only in rare cases where the defendant's conduct was egregious but resulted in only small or nominal compensatory damages (e.g. $10,000 in actual injury). When compensatory damages are already large ($1M+), a 1:1 or 2:1 ratio is often the constitutional ceiling.
What factors determine 'reprehensibility' under BMW v. Gore? +
1) Whether harm was physical rather than purely economic; 2) indifference to health and safety; 3) whether the victim was financially vulnerable; 4) repeated misconduct vs isolated incident; 5) intentional malice or deceit.

Frequently Asked Questions: Cost Segregation & Bonus Depreciation

What is a cost segregation study? +
A cost segregation study is an IRS-recognized engineering analysis that dissects real estate construction or purchase costs, reallocating components from 27.5-year or 39-year structural recovery periods into 5-year personal property (millwork, specialty electric, carpet) and 15-year land improvements (paving, sidewalks, fencing) eligible for immediate bonus depreciation.
Does cost segregation trigger depreciation recapture when sold? +
Yes, reclassified 5-year personal property is subject to Section 1245 depreciation recapture at ordinary income tax rates upon sale, while 15-year and 27.5-year real property is subject to Section 1250 recapture capped at 25%. However, this recapture is completely deferred if the investor executes an IRS Section 1031 like-kind exchange.
What size property makes cost segregation cost-effective? +
Generally, properties with a depreciable building basis of $500,000 or greater yield sufficient first-year tax write-offs ($50,000 to $150,000+) to produce an overwhelming 10x to 30x return on investment over the cost of the engineering study.

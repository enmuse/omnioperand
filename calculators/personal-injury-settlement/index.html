Frequently Asked Questions: Personal Injury Settlements

How do insurance adjusters calculate pain and suffering? +
Insurance adjusters commonly use the Multiplier Method: they sum total economic special damages (medical bills + lost wages) and multiply that number by a factor between 1.5x and 5.0x depending on injury severity, invasiveness of treatment (surgery vs. physical therapy), and permanency of impairment.
What is comparative negligence and how does it affect a settlement? +
Under comparative fault doctrines (e.g. California or Florida), the claimant's gross settlement is reduced by their percentage of fault. For example, in a $100,000 claim where the plaintiff is deemed 20% at fault, the maximum recoverable gross payout is reduced to $80,000.
Are personal injury settlements taxable? +
Under IRC Section 104(a)(2), compensatory damages received on account of personal physical injuries or physical sickness are 100% exempt from federal and state income taxes. Only punitive damages or interest on awards are taxable.

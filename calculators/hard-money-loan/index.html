Frequently Asked Questions: Hard Money & Bridge Financing

What is the 70% Rule in real estate flipping? +
The 70% Rule states that an investor should pay no more than 70% of the After-Repair Value (ARV) minus estimated repair costs: Maximum Allowable Offer (MAO) = (ARV · 0.70) - Rehab Budget. This ensures adequate margin for loan interest, closing fees, holding costs, and investor profit.
How does Loan-to-Cost (LTC) differ from Loan-to-ARV (LTARV)? +
Loan-to-Cost (LTC) caps financing based on actual capital deployed (typically 80% to 90% of purchase price + 100% of rehab budget). Loan-to-ARV (LTARV) caps the loan against the future appraised value after renovations (typically capped at 70% to 75% of ARV). Lenders lend the lesser of the two caps.
How are hard money loan payments structured? +
Most hard money loans are short-term (6 to 12 months) and require interest-only monthly payments with the entire principal due at maturity upon the sale or refinancing of the completed property.

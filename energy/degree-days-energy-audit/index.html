Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
HDD = Σ max(0, 65°F - T_mean) | Annual Heat BTU = UA_building × HDD × 24

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Thermal Envelope UA Factor

Building UA represents heat loss per degree Fahrenheit temperature difference. For a 2,400 sq ft home: Standard envelope UA ≈ 0.125 BTU/hr·sqft·°F × 2,400 = 300 BTU/hr·°F.

2
Annual Heating BTU Derivation

Q_heat = UA × HDD × 24 hrs/day. 300 × 4,200 × 24 = 30,240,000 BTU/year.

3
Therm Equivalency

1 Therm = 100,000 BTU. Assuming 85% AFUE furnace: Fuel Therms = (30,240,000 / 0.85) / 100,000 = 355.8 Therms.

4
Cooling Electric Requirement

Cooling BTU = UA × CDD × 24 = 300 × 1,400 × 24 = 10,080,000 BTU. With 15 SEER AC: 10,080,000 / (15 × 1000) = 672 kWh.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the baseline 65°F used for Degree Days? +
Building scientists established 65°F (18.3°C) as the standard baseline because typical internal heat gains (occupants, lighting, electronics, solar radiation) keep a home comfortable without heating until outdoor temperatures fall below 65°F.
How do degree days allow weather normalization? +
By dividing monthly utility bills by actual recorded degree days (Therms/HDD or kWh/CDD), energy auditors can determine whether a higher bill was caused by colder weather or declining HVAC equipment efficiency.

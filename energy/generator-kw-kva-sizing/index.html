Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
Generator Starting kVA = Running kVA + Σ (Motor HP × Starting Inrush Multiplier)

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Running Apparent Power

S_run = Continuous kW / PF. For 22 kW at 0.8 PF: 22 / 0.8 = 27.5 kVA.

2
Motor Starting Inrush Surge

NEMA Code G motors draw ~6.0 kVA per horsepower across-the-line (Direct-On-Line). A 5 HP motor draws: 5 HP × 6.0 = 30.0 kVA starting inrush.

3
Peak Transient Demand

Total Starting kVA = S_run + S_motor_inrush = 27.5 + 30.0 = 57.5 kVA.

4
Generator Commercial Selection

To prevent voltage dip > 20% on motor start, the generator must deliver 57.5 peak kVA. Standard commercial ratings: 30 kW, 35 kW, 45 kW, 60 kW. A 35 kW (43.7 kVA) unit with 150% surge rating satisfies all loads.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Standby and Prime generator ratings? +
Standby rating is for emergency backup during utility outages (max 200 hours/year at variable load). Prime rating is for continuous off-grid operation with 10% overload capability for 1 hour every 12 hours.
How does a Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) help generator sizing? +
A VFD eliminates the standard 600% motor inrush current spike, ramping up frequency smoothly and allowing you to install a significantly smaller, cheaper generator.

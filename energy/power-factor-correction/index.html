Governing Mathematical Formula:
Required kVAR = P × (tan(θ₁) - tan(θ₂)) | θ = arccos(PF)

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Trigonometric Phase Angles

θ₁ = arccos(0.76) = 40.54°. tan(40.54°) = 0.855. θ₂ = arccos(0.95) = 18.19°. tan(18.19°) = 0.329.

2
Capacitor Reactive Power Demand

Q_cap = P_kw × (tan(θ₁) - tan(θ₂)). For 250 kW: 250 × (0.8553 - 0.3287) = 131.65 kVAR.

3
Apparent Power Reduction

S₁ = 250 / 0.76 = 328.9 kVA. S₂ = 250 / 0.95 = 263.2 kVA. Freed electrical transformer capacity = 65.7 kVA.

4
3-Phase Amperage Relief

I = S / (√3 × V_ll). Original Current = 328,900 / (1.732 × 480) = 395.6A. Corrected Current = 263,200 / (1.732 × 480) = 316.5A (79.1A reduction).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do commercial utilities penalize low power factor? +
Low power factor forces utilities to supply larger apparent current (kVA) to deliver the same real power (kW), causing higher transmission I²R heat losses and requiring larger transformers and cabling.
Should I correct power factor past 0.98 or 1.0? +
Correcting beyond 0.98 is rarely cost-effective and risks leading power factor (capacitive over-correction), which causes hazardous harmonic resonance and voltage rise during light load periods.

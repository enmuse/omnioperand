Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Specific Fuel Consumption Standard

Diesel generators produce ~12 to 14 kWh per gallon of #2 diesel fuel. Propane produces ~7 to 8 kWh per gallon due to lower energy density (91,500 BTU/gal vs 138,500 BTU/gal for diesel).

2
Load-Dependent Consumption

For a 20 kW diesel generator at 50% load (10 kW delivered): Burn Rate ≈ 1.35 gallons per hour.

3
Runtime on Fuel Reserve

Runtime = Tank Capacity / Burn Rate. 50 gallons / 1.40 gal/hr = 35.7 hours of continuous power.

4
Levelized Fuel Energy Cost

Hourly Cost = 1.40 gal/hr × $3.85/gal = $5.39/hr. Cost per kWh = $5.39 / 10 kW = $0.539/kWh.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does a diesel generator burn fuel even with zero electrical load? +
Internal combustion diesel engines must maintain 1,800 RPM (or 3,600 RPM) to sustain 60 Hz AC grid frequency, consuming ~25% to 30% of full-load fuel just to overcome mechanical engine friction at zero load.
How does propane generator runtime compare to diesel? +
Propane has lower volumetric energy density than diesel. A propane generator burns roughly 40% to 50% more gallons per hour than an equivalent diesel unit to deliver the same kilowatt load, but propane stores indefinitely without going stale or gumming up.

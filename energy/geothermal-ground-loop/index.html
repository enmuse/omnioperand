Governing Mathematical Formula:
Total Vertical Borehole Feet = Heat Pump Tonnage × Borehole Ft/Ton (typically 150 - 200 ft/ton)

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Soil Thermal Heat Rejection

Ground heat exchange relies on thermal conductivity k. In damp soil (k = 1.4 BTU/hr·ft·°F), vertical boreholes require ~180 linear feet per ton of capacity.

2
Total Drilling Footage

Footage L = Tons × (Ft/Ton factor). For 4.0 Tons: 4.0 × 180 = 720 linear feet of vertical borehole.

3
Borehole Multi-Hole Array

To keep drilling practical and prevent ground freezing/thermal saturation, four 180-foot boreholes spaced 15 to 20 feet apart are drilled with HDPE U-tubes.

4
Hydraulic Fluid Volume

Standard 1-1/4" SDR-11 HDPE pipe holds ~0.090 gallons per linear foot of bore loop. 720 ft × 0.090 gal/ft = 64.8 gallons of 20% ethanol/water antifreeze mixture.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the typical vertical borehole drilling cost per foot? +
Turnkey geothermal vertical drilling and loop installation typically ranges from $25 to $40 per foot, including drilling, HDPE loop insertion, and thermal bentonite grouting.
Horizontal trench vs vertical boreholes: which is better? +
Horizontal trenches require much more open land (nearly an acre for a 4-ton system) and are affected by surface weather fluctuations, whereas vertical boreholes (150-300 ft deep) tap stable 50°F to 55°F deep bedrock temperatures and require minimal surface footprint.

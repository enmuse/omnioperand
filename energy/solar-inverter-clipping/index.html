Governing Mathematical Formula:
DC-to-AC Ratio (ILR) = Total DC Module Nameplate kW / Inverter Max Rated AC Output kW

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Inverter Loading Ratio (ILR)

ILR = P_dc / P_ac. For a 10.25 kW DC array with a 7.6 kW AC inverter: 10.25 / 7.6 = 1.348 (1.35).

2
Economics of Clipping

Panels rarely operate at STC (Standard Test Conditions) due to heat derating (~ -0.35%/°C), dust, and sun angle. Oversizing DC capacity by 20% to 35% keeps the inverter operating at its peak efficiency curve during morning and afternoon hours.

3
Clipping Losses vs Capital Savings

A 1.30 to 1.35 ILR results in only 0.5% to 2.0% annual energy clipping loss while saving $1,000+ compared to buying a larger inverter.

4
Max Continuous AC Backfeed

I_ac = (P_ac × 1000) / 240V. 7,600W / 240V = 31.67A, requiring a 40A backfeed circuit breaker (31.67 / 0.8 = 39.6A).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do solar installers oversize the DC array relative to the inverter? +
Oversizing the DC array (1.20 to 1.35 ILR) ensures the inverter operates closer to peak capacity for more hours each day, maximizing total kWh produced despite slight midday clipping.
Does clipping damage the inverter? +
No. Inverters use maximum power point tracking (MPPT) to shift their operating voltage along the IV curve, drawing only the exact current they are rated to process safely.

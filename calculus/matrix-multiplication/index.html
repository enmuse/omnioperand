Analytical Proof & Invariants

Governing Mathematical Formula:
det(A) = ad - bc | A⁻¹ = [1 / det(A)] · [ [d, -b], [-c, a] ]

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Geometric Area Scaling Factor

The determinant of a 2×2 matrix represents the signed factor by which area scales under the linear transformation: det(A) = ad - bc.

2
Cramer's Rule Adjugate Matrix Inversion

By the adjugate matrix formula: A⁻¹ = (1 / det A) · adj(A) = (1 / (ad - bc)) · [ [d, -b], [-c, a] ].

3
Singular Matrix Non-Invertibility

If det(A) = 0, the transformation collapses two-dimensional space onto a line or point; hence the matrix is singular and no inverse exists.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a zero determinant mean physically? +
A zero determinant means the linear transformation squashes space into a lower dimension (e.g., flattening an area into a line), meaning information is permanently lost and the operation cannot be reversed.
What are eigenvalues in linear algebra? +
Eigenvalues λ are scalar multipliers where multiplying the matrix by eigenvector v merely stretches or compresses v without changing its spatial direction: A · v = λ · v.

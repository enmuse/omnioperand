Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Primary Acidemia Identification

Blood pH = 7.30 (< 7.35 indicates acidemia). Bicarbonate HCO₃⁻ = 14 mEq/L (< 22 indicates metabolic driver).

2
Serum Anion Gap Derivation

Anion Gap = [Na⁺] - ([Cl⁻] + [HCO₃⁻]) = 140 - (102 + 14) = 140 - 116 = 24.0 mEq/L (Normal reference is 8 to 12 mEq/L).

3
Winter's Formula for Respiratory Compensation

Expected PaCO₂ = (1.5 × HCO₃⁻) + 8 ± 2 = (1.5 × 14) + 8 ± 2 = 21 + 8 ± 2 = 29 mmHg (range: 27 to 31 mmHg).

4
Clinical Integration

Because measured PaCO₂ (30 mmHg) matches Winter's formula (27-31 mmHg), the respiratory system is properly hyperventilating to blow off acidic CO₂ (compensating for primary High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis).

Frequently Asked Questions

What causes High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (HAGMA)? +
Common etiologies are memorized via the mnemonic GOLD MARK: Glycols, Oxoproline, L-lactate, D-lactate, Methanol, Aspirin (salicylates), Renal failure (uremia), and Ketoacidosis (diabetic, alcoholic, starvation).
Why must the Anion Gap be corrected for hypoalbuminemia? +
Albumin constitutes the majority of unmeasured plasma anions. For every 1.0 g/dL decrease in serum albumin below normal (4.0 g/dL), add 2.5 mEq/L to the calculated anion gap.

Governing Mathematical Formula:
pH = pKa + log₁₀([A⁻] / [HA]) | [A⁻] / [HA] = 10^(pH - pKa)

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Conjugate Base to Acid Ratio

Ratio = [A⁻] / [HA] = 0.150 M / 0.100 M = 1.50.

2
Logarithmic Ratio Component

log₁₀(1.50) = +0.176.

3
Henderson-Hasselbalch Solution pH

pH = pKa + log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA]) = 4.76 + 0.176 = 4.936 ≈ 4.94 pH.

4
Buffer Capacity Rule

A buffer is chemically effective within ±1.0 pH unit of its pKa (pH 3.76 to 5.76). Operating at pH 4.94 is within 0.18 units of the pKa, ensuring excellent resistance to both acid and base titration shocks.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens when [A⁻] equals [HA]? +
When conjugate base and weak acid concentrations are exactly equal, [A⁻]/[HA] = 1, and log₁₀(1) = 0. Therefore, pH = pKa. This represents the point of maximum buffer capacity.
What is the primary buffer system that maintains human physiological blood pH at 7.40? +
The carbonic acid-bicarbonate buffer system (H₂CO₃ / HCO₃⁻), which has a pKa of 6.1 and maintains a 20:1 ratio of bicarbonate to carbonic acid via respiratory CO₂ venting and renal bicarbonate retention.

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Experimental & Control Event Rates

Experimental Event Rate (EER) = 35 / 500 = 0.070 (7.0%). Control Event Rate (CER) = 70 / 500 = 0.140 (14.0%).

2
Relative Risk & Risk Reduction

Relative Risk (RR) = EER / CER = 0.070 / 0.140 = 0.50. Relative Risk Reduction (RRR) = 1 - 0.50 = 50.0% relative drop in adverse events.

3
Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR)

ARR = CER - EER = 14.0% - 7.0% = 7.0% absolute reduction in event frequency.

4
Number Needed to Treat (NNT)

NNT = 1 / ARR = 1 / 0.070 = 14.28 ≈ 15 patients. Treating 15 patients with the experimental drug prevents exactly 1 adverse clinical event compared to placebo.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR) more honest than Relative Risk Reduction (RRR)? +
Pharmaceutical marketing often highlights a '50% reduction' (RRR). However, if baseline risk drops from 2 in 10,000 to 1 in 10,000, the absolute benefit is only 0.01%, requiring 10,000 patients to be treated to prevent one event.
What is an ideal Number Needed to Treat (NNT)? +
An NNT of 1 means every single treated patient benefits. In clinical medicine, an NNT between 5 and 20 for life-threatening conditions (e.g. statins for secondary prevention of myocardial infarction) represents a major therapeutic success.

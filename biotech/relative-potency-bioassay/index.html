Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
Relative Potency = (EC50_std / EC50_test) · 100% | M = log10(EC50_std) - log10(EC50_test) | Parallelism = β_test / β_std

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. Relative Potency Formulation

In parallel-line bioassay models (USP <1032>), the test article is assumed to act as a dilution of the reference standard: Relative Potency = (EC50_reference / EC50_test) × 100%.

2
2. Parallelism Fundamental Invariant

For relative potency to be valid, the two dose-response curves must be parallel (same mechanism of action). The slope ratio β_test / β_std must lie within the equivalence margin [0.80, 1.25].

3
3. Fieller's Theorem Confidence Limits

Because relative potency is a ratio of two normally distributed random variables, standard linear error propagation fails. Fieller's theorem provides the mathematically exact asymmetric 95% confidence bounds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is relative potency calculated as standard / test? +
If a test sample requires a lower EC50 to achieve the same 50% biological effect as the standard, it is more potent. Thus, dividing standard EC50 by test EC50 correctly produces a relative potency > 100%.
What is the USP requirement for bioassay parallelism? +
USP <1032> requires that the dose-response curves of test and reference articles have identical upper asymptotes, lower asymptotes, and slope (slope ratio between 0.80 and 1.25).
What is Fieller's theorem? +
Fieller's theorem is a statistical formula for calculating confidence limits on the ratio of two parameter estimates, preventing distortion caused by dividing two random variables.

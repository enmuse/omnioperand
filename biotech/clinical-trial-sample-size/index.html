Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
N_arm = 2 · (Z_α/2 + Z_β)² · p̄(1 - p̄) / (p₁ - p₂)²

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Standard Normal Critical Values

For two-tailed α = 0.05: Z_α/2 = 1.960. For 80% statistical power (β = 0.20): Z_β = 0.842. (Z_α/2 + Z_β)² = (1.960 + 0.842)² = (2.802)² = 7.851.

2
Pooled Variance Proportions

p₁ = 0.20, p₂ = 0.35. Pooled mean p̄ = (0.20 + 0.35) / 2 = 0.275. Variance factor = 2 · p̄(1 - p̄) = 2 × 0.275 × 0.725 = 0.39875.

3
Per-Arm Sample Size Equation

N_arm = [7.851 × 0.39875] / (0.35 - 0.20)² = 3.1306 / (0.15)² = 3.1306 / 0.0225 = 139.1 ≈ 138 patients per arm.

4
Attrition & Dropout Buffer

Total needed = 138 × 2 arms = 276 evaluable patients. With 15% dropout: Total Enrolled = 276 / (1 - 0.15) = 324.7 ≈ 326 total patients to recruit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the consequence of an underpowered clinical trial? +
An underpowered study (e.g. 50% power) has a high probability of committing a Type II error (false negative), causing a genuinely effective, lifesaving drug to fail statistical significance and be abandoned.
Why is statistical power typically set to 80% instead of 99%? +
Increasing power from 80% to 99% quadruples the required patient sample size, dramatically increasing clinical trial cost and multi-year patient enrollment timelines.

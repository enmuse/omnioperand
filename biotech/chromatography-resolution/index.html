Governing Mathematical Formula:
N = 5.545 · (t_r / W_0.5)² | R_s = 1.18 · (t_r2 - t_r1) / (W_0.5,1 + W_0.5,2) | HETP = (L · 1000) / N

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. USP Theoretical Plate Count (N)

Under USP <621> and Ph. Eur. 2.2.46, column efficiency is calculated from peak width at half-height (W_0.5): N = 5.545 × (t_r / W_0.5)². This eliminates baseline noise ambiguity.

2
2. Chromatographic Resolution (Rs)

Resolution quantifies separation between adjacent peaks: R_s = 1.18 × (t_r2 - t_r1) / (W_0.5,1 + W_0.5,2). Complete baseline separation requires R_s ≥ 1.50.

3
3. Van Deemter Plate Height (HETP)

Height Equivalent to a Theoretical Plate measures packaging efficiency: HETP = Length / N. For sub-3µm particle columns, HETP is typically 10 to 20 µm.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good theoretical plate count for an HPLC column? +
A standard 150 mm C18 column with 5 µm particles should generate 10,000 to 14,000 plates. Modern UHPLC columns (1.7–2.7 µm particles) achieve 25,000 to 40,000+ plates.
What is the minimum acceptable resolution (Rs) in pharmaceutical QC? +
USP <621> requires a minimum resolution of Rs ≥ 1.50 for baseline baseline separation between critical pairs (API and adjacent process impurities).
What is HETP? +
HETP (Height Equivalent to a Theoretical Plate) is column length divided by plate count. Smaller HETP values indicate higher chromatographic separation efficiency.

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Centrifugal Acceleration Formula

Centrifugal acceleration a = ω² · r, where angular velocity ω = 2π · RPM / 60. Normalizing to Earth gravity (g = 9.80665 m/s²) yields the constant 1.118 × 10⁻⁵.

2
RCF Calculation

RCF = 1.118 × 10⁻⁵ × Radius (8.5 cm) × (14,000 RPM)² = 1.118 × 10⁻⁵ × 8.5 × 196,000,000 = 18,624.76 ≈ 18,625 ×g.

3
Peripheral Speed

Linear Velocity v = (2π · 8.5 cm / 100) × (14,000 / 60) = 0.534 m × 233.33 rev/s = 124.6 m/s (448.6 km/h).

4
Protocol Reproducibility Rule

Scientific protocols must always specify g-force (RCF), never RPM alone. Running 14,000 RPM on a larger 12 cm rotor produces 26,295 ×g (+41% higher force), potentially lysing delicate organelles.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is it dangerous to report centrifugation speeds in RPM? +
RPM describes only how fast the rotor spins, ignoring the physical size of the rotor. Two centrifuges running at the same 5,000 RPM will exert drastically different forces if one has an 8 cm rotor (2,236 ×g) and the other has a 16 cm rotor (4,472 ×g).
What is the difference between r_min, r_av, and r_max in centrifuge rotors? +
r_min is the distance from the spindle to the liquid surface, r_max is the distance to the tube tip, and r_av is the midpoint. RCF is typically reported at r_max (pellet force).

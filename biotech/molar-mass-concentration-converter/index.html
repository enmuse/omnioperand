Governing Mathematical Formula:
Mass Conc (ng/mL) = Conc (µM) · MW (g/mol) | Mass (µg) = Conc (µg/mL) · Volume (mL)

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
1. The 1:1 Identity Between µM and ng/mL

By dimensional analysis: 1 µM = 10⁻⁶ mol/L = 10⁻⁹ mol/mL. Multiplying by MW (g/mol = 10⁹ ng/mol) yields: Conc (ng/mL) = Conc (µM) × MW (g/mol). For example, 1 µM of a 500 g/mol compound is exactly 500 ng/mL.

2
2. Unit Conversion Ladder

1 µg/mL = 1,000 ng/mL = 1 mg/L = 1 ppm. 1 nM of a substance equals (MW / 1,000) ng/mL.

3
3. Avogadro Molecular Particle Count

Total moles in volume V (liters) is n = Molarity × V. Total particle count is N = n × N_A, where N_A = 6.02214076 × 10²³ molecules/mol.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you convert micromolar (µM) to ng/mL? +
Simply multiply the micromolar concentration by the molecular weight of the substance in g/mol: ng/mL = µM × MW. For example, 10 µM of a drug with MW = 400 g/mol equals 4,000 ng/mL (4 µg/mL).
Why is molecular weight required to convert µM to ng/mL? +
Micromolar is a count of molecules per liter, while ng/mL is physical mass per milliliter. Because different molecules have different masses, you cannot convert between them without knowing molecular weight.
Is 1 µg/mL the same as 1 mg/L? +
Yes. 1 µg/mL is mathematically identical to 1 mg/L and 1 part-per-million (ppm) in aqueous solutions.

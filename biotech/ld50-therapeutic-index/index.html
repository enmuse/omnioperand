Analytical Proof & Invariants

Machine Precision
Governing Mathematical Formula:
Therapeutic Index (TI) = LD₅₀ / ED₅₀ | Margin of Safety (MOS) = LD₁ / ED₉₉

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Therapeutic Index (TI) Derivation

Therapeutic Index = LD₅₀ / ED₅₀ = 450.0 mg/kg / 25.0 mg/kg = 18.00. The median lethal dose is 18 times higher than the median effective dose.

2
Margin of Safety (Certain Safety Factor MOS)

MOS = LD₁ / ED₉₉ = 180.0 mg/kg / 60.0 mg/kg = 3.00. Because MOS > 1.0, the dose required to cure 99% of patients is 3 times lower than the dose that causes toxicity in even 1%.

3
Preclinical Safety Significance

A drug with TI > 10 and MOS > 1.0 represents an exceptionally favorable safety profile, qualifying for progression into Phase I First-in-Human clinical trials.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Margin of Safety (MOS) safer than Therapeutic Index (TI)? +
TI compares median 50% points, which ignores the slope of dose-response curves. If the toxicity curve has a very steep slope, a drug with an apparently safe TI of 10 might have an LD₁ lower than the ED₉₉ (meaning patients could die before reaching 99% efficacy). MOS tests the extreme tails (LD₁ vs ED₉₉).
What is an example of a drug with a Narrow Therapeutic Index (NTI)? +
Digoxin, Warfarin, Lithium, and Theophylline have narrow therapeutic indices (TI < 2.0). Small dosage variations or drug-drug interactions can quickly cause severe clinical toxicity.

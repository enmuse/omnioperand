Frequently Asked Questions

Why is effect size superior to p-values in clinical trials? +
A p-value merely tells you whether an effect is statistically distinguishable from zero; with a huge sample size, even a clinically trivial difference becomes statistically significant. Cohen's d measures the actual magnitude of the clinical improvement in standard deviation units.
When must Hedges' g be used instead of Cohen's d? +
When group sample size is small (n < 20 per group), Cohen's d systematically overestimates the true population effect size. Hedges' g applies an exact correction factor to eliminate this positive bias.

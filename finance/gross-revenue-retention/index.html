Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Gross Revenue Preservation Principle

GRR = [(Starting MRR - Churn - Contraction) / Starting MRR] × 100%. Expansion MRR is strictly omitted from the numerator.

2
GRR Upper Bound Ceiling

By mathematical definition, GRR can never exceed 100.0%. A GRR of 90%+ indicates that customers stick with the platform even without factoring in upgrades.

3
The Leaky Bucket Test

A business with 120% NRR but 75% GRR has a severe underlying product defect that is masked only by aggressive upselling to a small fraction of customers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between GRR and NRR? +
GRR measures the percentage of recurring revenue retained from existing customers excluding all expansion and upsells. NRR includes expansion. GRR can never exceed 100%, whereas NRR can.
What is a good GRR benchmark for enterprise software? +
For enterprise B2B SaaS, top-quartile GRR is 90% to 95%. For mid-market SaaS, 85% to 90% is solid. For SMB SaaS, 75% to 85% is typical.

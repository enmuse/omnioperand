Mathematical Rigor & Closed-Form Proofs

1 Standard Fixed-Rate Amortization Derivation

▼

A mortgage is an ordinary annuity where the present value of all future monthly payments equals the original loan principal P discounted at monthly interest rate i = r / 12:

P = ∑ (from k = 1 to n) [ M / (1 + i)k ]

Using the finite geometric series formula ∑k=1ⁿ xk = x(1 - xⁿ) / (1 - x) with x = (1 + i)⁻¹:

P = M · [ 1 - (1 + i)-n ] / i = M · [ (1 + i)n - 1 ] / [ i · (1 + i)n ]

Solving for the monthly payment M yields the classical fixed-rate payment formula:

M = P · [ i · (1 + i)n ] / [ (1 + i)n - 1 ]

2 Borrowing Power Inversion & Term Resolution

▼

To compute how much home a buyer can finance for a given monthly budget M, we invert the payment equation directly:

P = M · [ (1 + i)n - 1 ] / [ i · (1 + i)n ]

Similarly, if principal P and payment M are known, the remaining amortization horizon in months n is resolved via natural logarithms:

n = -ln(1 - i · P / M) / ln(1 + i)

This ensures that our solver can run backward from any target constraint with machine precision.

3 Accelerated Payoff & Compounding Interest Differential

▼

For any given payment month k, the monthly interest charge is strictly determined by the preceding unpaid balance:

Ik = Bk-1 · i

When an extra principal payment E is added, the new balance reduces by Bk = Bk-1 - (M - Ik + E). Because every dollar of extra principal permanently eliminates future interest accrual across all remaining months, the interest savings compound exponentially.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the mathematical formula for a fixed-rate mortgage payment? +
The monthly payment M is derived from the ordinary annuity present value formula:
M = P · [i(1 + i)ⁿ] / [(1 + i)ⁿ - 1]
where P is loan principal, i is monthly interest rate (APR / 12), and n is total payment periods (Years × 12).
What is the difference between APR and interest rate? +
The interest rate is the pure percentage charged on the principal balance each year. The Annual Percentage Rate (APR) includes the interest rate plus lender origination fees, discount points, closing costs, and private mortgage insurance (PMI), reflecting the true annual cost of credit.
How does extra monthly principal accelerate mortgage payoff? +
Because monthly interest is calculated strictly on the remaining principal balance (Balance × i), any extra payment directly reduces principal. Making one additional monthly payment each year typically shortens a 30-year mortgage by 4 to 6 years and saves tens of thousands in compounding interest.
Can this calculator determine how much home I can afford? +
Yes! By typing your desired monthly payment directly into the "Base Monthly Payment (M)" field, OmniOperand immediately inverts the annuity formula to show the maximum loan principal P your payment can support: P = M · [(1 + i)ⁿ - 1] / [i(1 + i)ⁿ].

Governing Mathematical Formula:
Breakeven Months = Total Closing Costs / (Current Payment - New Payment)

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Monthly Amortization Derivation

Monthly payments M are computed using the annuity equation: M = P · [i(1 + i)ⁿ] / [(1 + i)ⁿ - 1], where i = r / 12 and n = years × 12.

2
Monthly Cash Flow Differential

Monthly payment savings ΔM = M_current - M_new. If ΔM > 0, the homeowner captures immediate monthly liquidity.

3
Capital Recovery & Breakeven Horizon

The exact time in months to recover closing costs C is: T_breakeven = C / ΔM. If the homeowner remains in the home longer than T_breakeven, refinancing yields positive economic surplus.

4
Net Lifetime Wealth Impact

Net lifetime savings = (Total remaining payments under old loan) - (Total payments under new loan + closing costs).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is considered a good breakeven period for refinancing? +
Financial planners typically target a breakeven timeline of under 36 months (3 years). If you plan to remain in the property longer than the breakeven threshold, refinancing is financially accretive.
Should I roll closing costs into the loan balance? +
Rolling closing costs into the new principal avoids out-of-pocket expenses, but increases the principal balance P, raising both your monthly payment and total lifetime interest paid.
Can refinancing to a 15-year loan save more money? +
Yes! While a 15-year term increases your monthly payment due to faster principal amortization, it significantly reduces total lifetime interest by cutting interest accrual time in half.

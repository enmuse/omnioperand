Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Federal FUTA Tax Rate and Cap

FUTA is an employer-paid tax (never withheld from employee wages). The gross rate is 6.0%, but employers paying on-time state unemployment receive a 5.4% credit, resulting in an effective net rate of 0.6% on the first $7,000 of wages: $7,000 × 0.6% = $42.00 per employee per year.

2
State SUTA Calculations

SUTA Tax = min(Wages, State Wage Base) × SUTA Experience Rate. For 8 workers at a $10,000 state cap and 2.7% rate: 8 × $10,000 × 2.7% = $2,160.

3
Employer Cost Consolidation

Total Tax = FUTA ($336) + SUTA ($2,160) = $2,496/year ($312/employee).

Frequently Asked Questions

Do employees pay FUTA or SUTA tax? +
In 47 out of 50 states, unemployment taxes are paid 100% by the employer with zero employee deductions. Only Alaska, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania require a modest employee SUTA co-contribution.
How can an employer lower their SUTA tax rate? +
SUTA rates are based on an employer's 'experience rating.' Minimizing employee layoffs and turnover keeps your experience rating low, dropping state tax rates from 8%+ down to under 1%.

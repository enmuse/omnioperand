Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation

1
Acid-Test Asset Isolation

Quick Assets = Cash + Marketable Securities + Net Receivables. Excludes physical inventory and prepaid insurance.

2
Quick Ratio Derivation

Quick Ratio = Quick Assets / Current Liabilities.

3
Solvency Invariant

A Quick Ratio ≥ 1.0 proves that the firm possesses sufficient instant liquidity to pay off 100% of current debt immediately without needing to sell a single unit of inventory.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the Quick Ratio called the 'Acid-Test'? +
The name originates from early gold prospectors who applied nitric acid to metal samples: real gold was impervious, while base metals dissolved. In finance, the quick ratio tests whether a business survives an immediate financial crisis.
What is an acceptable Quick Ratio? +
A Quick Ratio of 1.0 or higher is considered healthy for most commercial enterprises. In software and services with minimal inventory, quick ratios often match current ratios.

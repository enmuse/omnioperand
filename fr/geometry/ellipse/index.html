Démonstration Mathématique Étape par Étape

1
Affine Stretching Area Integral

An ellipse x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 is the linear transformation of a unit circle x² + y² = 1 stretched by factor a horizontally and b vertically: Area = ∫∫ dx dy = π · a · b.

2
Ramanujan's Second Perimeter Formula

Because the exact perimeter requires an incomplete elliptic integral of the second kind E(e), Srinivasa Ramanujan formulated the famously accurate closed-form approximation: P ≈ π(a + b)[1 + 3h / (10 + √(4 - 3h))], where h = (a - b)² / (a + b)².

3
Foci and Orbital Eccentricity

Linear focal distance c = √(a² - b²). First eccentricity e = c / a = √(1 - b²/a²). When e = 0, the ellipse collapses to a perfect circle; as e ➔ 1, it elongates into a parabola.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is there no simple closed-form formula for the perimeter of an ellipse? +
Unlike a circle where curvature is constant, an ellipse's curvature varies continuously. Integrating the arc length differential ds = √[dx² + dy²] yields a non-elementary elliptic integral that cannot be expressed with basic algebraic functions.
What is the error of Ramanujan's second perimeter formula? +
Ramanujan's second approximation has an extraordinary maximum relative error of less than 0.0015% even for extremely flattened ellipses with eccentricity near 1.

Mathematical Rigor & Closed-Form Formulations

1 Net Financed Amount & Trade-in Tax Credit

▼

When a trade-in vehicle T is applied, the taxable purchase price is reduced to V - T in qualifying jurisdictions:

Tax = (V - T) · (s / 100)

The total out-the-door price including documentation fees F is:

Total Out-The-Door = V + Tax + F = V · (1 + s/100) - T · (s/100) + F

Subtracting upfront cash down payment D and trade-in value T gives the net principal financed P:

P = V · (1 + s/100) + F - D - T · (1 + s/100)

2 Monthly Payment & Budget Inversion Formulation

▼

Given monthly interest rate i = r / 1200 and term N months:

M = P · [ i · (1 + i)N ] / [ (1 + i)N - 1 ]

Inverting for affordable sticker price V from target monthly budget M:

Pmax = M · [ (1 + i)N - 1 ] / [ i · (1 + i)N ]
V = [ Pmax + D + T · (1 + s/100) - F ] / (1 + s/100)

Întrebări Frecvente

How does a trade-in affect auto loan sales tax? +
In the majority of US states, trade-in equity reduces the taxable purchase price of the vehicle. For example, if a car costs $40,000 and your trade-in is worth $10,000, you only pay sales tax on the remaining $30,000 net difference, saving substantial tax dollars.
What is the 20/4/10 rule for car buying? +
The 20/4/10 rule is a popular personal finance guideline: (1) Put down at least 20% in cash or trade-in; (2) Finance the vehicle for no longer than 4 years (48 months); and (3) Keep total monthly vehicle expenses (payment, insurance, fuel) below 10% of gross monthly income.
Is GAP insurance necessary? +
Guaranteed Asset Protection (GAP) insurance is highly recommended if you make a small down payment (less than 20%) or choose a loan term of 60 months or longer. If the vehicle is totaled, GAP insurance covers the difference between the car's depreciated market value and what you still owe the lender.
Why are longer auto loans (72 or 84 months) riskier? +
While 72- and 84-month terms lower the monthly payment, vehicles depreciate faster than the principal balance amortizes in the first 3 years. This creates negative equity ("underwater" loans) where you owe more than the car is worth, and significantly increases total interest paid over the life of the loan.
